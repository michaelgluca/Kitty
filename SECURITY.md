# Security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

**Please do not open a public issue for a security problem.**

Use GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on this repository:
**Security → Report a vulnerability**. It is free, it is private, and it goes only to the maintainer.

Please include what you found, how to reproduce it, which version or commit you tested, and what you
think the impact is. A proof of concept helps. If you are not sure whether something counts, report
it anyway.

### What to expect

| | |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement | Within 7 days |
| Initial assessment | Within 14 days |
| Fix or a plan with dates | Within 30 days for anything rated high or critical |

This is a single-maintainer project, so those are honest targets rather than a contractual SLA. If
you have not heard back within the acknowledgement window, please escalate by opening a public issue
that says only that you are awaiting a response to a private report — no details.

Coordinated disclosure is preferred: please give us a chance to ship a fix before publishing. There
is no bug bounty. Reporters are credited in the advisory and the changelog unless they ask not to be.

## Threat model

Kitty G is built for people whose safety may depend on it, so the threat model is unusual and worth
stating explicitly. It follows **OWASP MASVS v2.1.0**, targeting the **MAS-L2 + MAS-P** profiles.

The adversaries we design against:

1. **A lost or stolen phone.** Offline attacker, possibly with forensic tooling.
2. **Someone with physical access to the unlocked phone — typically an abusive partner.** This is the
   dominant threat and it shapes most of the design. MAS-L2's stated attacker model covers exactly
   this: "a third party with or without physical access".
3. **A malicious fork, contributor or dependency.** A modified build that looks like this app but
   exfiltrates location is a physical safety risk, not just a supply chain one.

### What we deliberately do not defend against

**We do not attempt anti-tampering, anti-debugging or obfuscation** (MASVS-RESILIENCE-2, -3, -4).
MASVS's own "Transparency and Open Audit Perspective" section states that resilience measures may be
*inappropriate* for public-interest applications and recommends open source and verifiable design
instead. We agree. This app's integrity should be checkable by anyone, not hidden.

**Certificate pinning is not applicable** (MASVS-NETWORK-2). There is no endpoint under our control
to pin to, because there is no backend.

### What the app cannot do, and does not claim

Kitty G cannot hide itself. Its name remains visible in Spotlight, Settings, App Library, Screen
Time, Storage and the App Store purchase history. Sent messages remain in Messages; calls remain in
Recents. **A report that "an abuser could see the app is installed" is a known and documented
limitation, not a vulnerability** — but a report that the app *claims* otherwise anywhere in its UI
or marketing very much is one, and we want to hear it.

## In scope

- Anything that causes data to leave the device other than through an action the user took.
- Any weakening of Keychain or Data Protection classes, or data stored where it should not be.
- Personal data appearing in logs, crash reports, the app-switcher snapshot or a backup.
- A safety-critical path that fails silently, or that presents placeholder data as real — for example
  a location that is not the user's actual location.
- Anything that would make the "Data Not Collected" privacy label untrue.
- Incorrect emergency information: a wrong phone number, a dead or hijacked link, or guidance that
  contradicts the issuing body. **Treat these as security issues — they carry the same real-world
  consequences and we would rather over-triage them.**
- Supply chain: a dependency appearing where there should be none, or a CI guard that can be bypassed.

## Out of scope

- Findings that require a jailbroken device, given the stated resilience position above.
- Missing obfuscation or anti-debugging.
- The app being discoverable on the device, as described above.
- Denial of service against third-party public services such as `data.police.uk`.
- Reports generated by automated scanners with no demonstrated impact.

## Supported versions

The latest App Store release and the `main` branch. There are no long-term support branches.
